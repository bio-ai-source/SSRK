We use PBMC~3k~\cite{zhengMassivelyParallelDigital2017,hatjeBescaPBMC3k2020}, MNIST~\cite{lecunGradientbasedLearningApplied1998}, Fashion-MNIST~\cite{xiaoFashionMNISTNovelImage2017}, and UCI HAR~\cite{anguitaPublicDomainDataset2013}; preprocessing, splits, and selection budgets are listed in Table~\ref{tab:settings}. The primary protocol uses 20 matched runs per dataset (bootstrap refits for PBMC; runs that differ only in training randomness otherwise) with shared budgets and evaluation, 95\% bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) bootstrap intervals, and Holm-adjusted sign-randomization tests within each dataset--metric family (Table~\ref{tab:paired_summary}). Across the 70 comparisons, SSRK is significantly better in 52 and worse in 4; the remaining 14 show no significant difference.
In the matched protocol of 20 bootstrap refits, SSRK's ARI (0.355) is significantly higher than that of CAE, GAEFS, and SSFS (and of Variance, nearly uninformative on this per-gene standardized matrix) and not significantly different from that of STG ($-0.009$, 95\% BCa interval $[-0.035,0.025]$); its NMI is not significantly different from that of SSFS ($-0.001$, Holm $p=0.68$), its coverage score is significantly higher than that of all five baselines, and its top-100 set is the most stable of the six methods (Jaccard index 0.772 against 0.756 for SSFS, marker Jaccard index 1.000; Appendix~\ref{sec:pbmc_app}). Among the baselines, STG has a higher NMI, and SSFS retrieves 0.8 more markers on average.
Across 20 matched runs, scored by a multilayer perceptron (MLP) averaged over five fixed initializations, its accuracy and macro-F1 (0.949/0.948) are significantly above those of STG, CAE, GAEFS, and SSFS and not significantly different from those of Variance (accuracy gap $-0.0004$, Holm $p=0.69$); none of its 20 selections contains an appended column, and its domain imbalance is significantly lower than that of Variance, STG, CAE, and SSFS.
Table~\ref{tab:image} reports the MNIST five-run comparison; SSRK leads on ARI and NMI. In the MNIST matched protocol of 20 runs, evaluated on pixels only, SSRK's ARI and NMI are significantly higher than those of all four learned baselines and not significantly different from those of Variance, and its coverage efficiency is significantly higher than that of all five baselines. On Fashion-MNIST, SSRK's ARI is significantly higher than that of the four learned baselines and not significantly different from that of Variance; the two significant deficits on this dataset are an NMI 0.003 below Variance and a coverage efficiency 0.090 below STG (Table~\ref{tab:paired_summary}).
All baselines use the selection budgets of Table~\ref{tab:settings}; the matched protocol shares data, training randomness, and evaluation across methods. Variance ranks empirical column variances. STG~\cite{yamadaFeatureSelectionUsing2020} trains Gaussian gates $z_j=\min\{1,\max\{0,0.5+\mu_j+0.5\epsilon_j\}\}$ with a reconstruction loss and a sparsity penalty and ranks the corresponding deterministic gates. CAE~\cite{balinConcreteAutoencodersDifferentiable2019} uses Concrete weights $\operatorname{softmax}((\ell+g)/T)$ in the reconstruction input and ranks the logits while annealing $T$. For GAEFS~\cite{tanGAEFSSelfsupervisedGraph2024}, we learn feature gates against a spectral embedding of a 10-nearest-neighbor graph, with an additional reconstruction term outside MNIST and embedding alignment for PBMC and MNIST. For SSFS~\cite{segalSpectralSelfsupervisedFeature2024}, we rank feature-wise between-/within-cluster sums of squares under $k$-means pseudo-labels, selecting $k$ by the silhouette score on a PCA embedding. On the per-gene standardized PBMC matrix, Variance is nearly uninformative (ARI 0.048).
Unsupervised feature selection is widely used as a discovery tool, yet most methods return absolute rankings rather than comparisons with feature-wise matched references. We introduce Self-Supervised Reconstruction Knockoffs (SSRK), a proxy-selection framework for unlabeled data. For controlled discovery, a data-independent partition defines a candidate block and a held-out target block, and knockoffs are generated only for the candidates. A mixture-only gated predictor, tied complemented logits, and swap-equivariant optimization produce a flip-sign statistic for the predeclared target; without a target block, the same gated predictor reconstructs the candidates themselves and ranks them against matched-marginal references. We formalize knockoff-relative masked information, show that a same-vector population contrast degenerates under exact knockoffs but measures cross-coordinate information under matched-marginal references, derive a positive-entropy local gate fixed point and a bounded-class concentration result, and specialize exact and defect-robust Model-X bounds on the false discovery rate (FDR). In an exact Gaussian fixed-target study with six signal amplitudes (50 datasets each), the mean false discovery proportion (FDP) stays at or below $q\in\{0.05,0.10,0.15\}$ up to Monte Carlo error, while power at $q=0.10$ rises from 0.167 to 1.000; 100 all-null datasets yield no nonempty selection at $q=0.10$, and retraining on swapped data reproduces the sign-flipped statistic exactly in every checked run. On peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC~3k), MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and UCI Human Activity Recognition, SSRK rankings are significantly better than the corresponding baseline in 52 of 70 matched 20-run comparisons and worse in 4 (Holm adjustment within each dataset--metric family).
